From Research Project to Open Source Ecosystem

原文：[From Research Project to Open Source Ecosystem: Bring Your Academic PyTorch Project to PyTorchCon NA](https://pytorch.org/blog/from-research-project-to-open-source-ecosystem-bring-your-academic-pytorch-project-to-pytorchcon-na/)

摘要

本期讨论 PyTorch 基金会面向学术项目的“从学术走向采用”工作坊，帮助优秀研究代码成长为可持续的开源社区项目。节目介绍征集对象、评选标准、闪电演讲和导师办公时间，以及文档、治理、可复现性、安全和维护等常见缺口。符合条件的项目需要使用 PyTorch 或其他 PyTorch 基金会托管项目，并在 2026 年 9 月 30 日太平洋时间午夜前提交。核心信息是：项目不必完美成熟，只要有价值、有潜力，就值得申请帮助。

对话

Ava: What happens when a useful PyTorch research project outlives the paper that created it?

Ava：一项有用的 PyTorch 研究项目，在催生它的论文发表后仍持续发展，会面临什么？

That’s the question behind a new academic workshop at PyTorch Conference North America 2026.

这正是 2026 年北美 PyTorch 大会新设学术工作坊要探讨的问题。

Brian: Right.

Brian：没错。

The workshop is trying to help projects make a difficult jump: from a research artifact to a sustainable open source project that a wider community can use and maintain.

工作坊希望帮助项目跨过一道难关：从研究成果变成更广泛的社区能够使用和维护的可持续开源项目。

Ava: So this is for university labs and student teams whose code works, but whose project may still depend on one graduating student or one person who knows the release process.

Ava：也就是说，它面向代码已经能用，但项目可能仍依赖某位即将毕业的学生，或唯一懂发布流程的人的大学实验室和学生团队。

Brian: Exactly.

Brian：正是。

The PyTorch Foundation OSPO and Academic Outreach Working Group wants to bridge that gap.

PyTorch 基金会的 OSPO 与学术推广工作组希望弥合这道鸿沟。

OSPO means Open Source Program Office.

OSPO 是“开源项目办公室”（Open Source Program Office）的缩写。

The group is inviting academic PyTorch projects to apply for a Day 0 workshop focused on education and community adoption.

该工作组邀请学术界的 PyTorch 项目申请参加聚焦教育和社区采用的 Day 0 工作坊。

Ava: Let’s start with the basic opportunity. What does a selected team actually get?

Ava：先说说最基本的机会。入选团队能得到什么？

Brian: Eight selected projects will give short lightning talks during the workshop.

Brian：八个入选项目将在工作坊上做简短的闪电演讲。

Each talk is five minutes, and presenters must attend in person.

每场演讲五分钟，演讲者必须到场参加。

After that, the teams join a hands-on workshop and mentor office hours.

之后，团队将参加动手实践工作坊和导师答疑时段。

Ava: Office hours with whom?

Ava：导师都有谁？

Brian: With maintainers, open source program managers, people who support open source ecosystems in industry and research organizations, open source contributors, and members of the broader PyTorch community.

Brian：包括项目维护者、开源项目经理、在企业和研究机构支持开源生态的人士、开源贡献者，以及更广泛的 PyTorch 社区成员。

Ava: So it’s not just a showcase.

Ava：所以这不只是成果展示。

Teams bring their real repositories and work on them with mentors.

团队会带着自己的真实代码仓库，与导师一起改进。

Brian: That’s the idea. The article describes a move from show-and-tell to show-and-build.

Brian：就是这个意思。文章说，活动要从“展示和讲解”走向“展示和共建”。

The goal is for each team to leave with a prioritized set of next steps.

目标是让每个团队离开时，都有一份按优先级排列的后续行动清单。

Ava: What kind of projects are they looking for? Is this only about a new model architecture?

Ava：他们想找什么样的项目？只限新的模型架构吗？

Brian: No. They want projects with technical value beyond one experiment or one paper.

Brian：不是。他们希望项目的技术价值能超越单次实验或一篇论文。

That could be a library or developer tool, a research framework for a specific domain, or a compiler, runtime, profiling, distributed training, inference, or optimization project.

可以是库或开发者工具、特定领域的研究框架，也可以是编译器、运行时、性能分析、分布式训练、推理或优化项目。

Ava: That’s a pretty wide range.

Ava：范围相当广。

Brian: It is.

Brian：是的。

The call also includes work in computer vision, natural language processing, robotics, scientific computing, healthcare, responsible AI, and accessibility.

征集范围还包括计算机视觉、自然语言处理、机器人、科学计算、医疗健康、负责任的 AI 和无障碍领域的项目。

Evaluation and benchmarking frameworks count too, as do reproducibility toolkits, research infrastructure, and educational tools.

评估与基准测试框架、可复现性工具包、研究基础设施和教学工具也都在范围内。

Ava: And the project has to connect to the PyTorch ecosystem.

Ava：而且项目必须与 PyTorch 生态有关。

Brian: Yes. It needs to use PyTorch or another PyTorch Foundation hosted project.

Brian：对。项目需要使用 PyTorch 或 PyTorch 基金会托管的其他项目。

The article names DeepSpeed, Helion, Ray, Safetensors, and vLLM as examples of those hosted projects.

文章举出的托管项目包括 DeepSpeed、Helion、Ray、Safetensors 和 vLLM。

Ava: Could it be an implementation that accompanies a major research paper?

Ava：重大研究论文的配套实现可以申请吗？

Brian: Yes, if it has a life beyond the paper.

Brian：可以，只要它在论文之外还有持续发展的价值。

They also welcome projects that connect these projects with other parts of the open source AI stack, and they say they’re open to ideas they haven’t thought of yet.

他们也欢迎将这些项目与开源 AI 技术栈其他部分连接起来的项目，并表示乐于接受尚未想到的新点子。

Ava: That last part sounds encouraging.

Ava：最后这点挺让人鼓舞。

A team doesn’t have to force its work into a fixed category.

团队不必硬把自己的工作塞进固定类别。

Brian: Right. The larger message is that you don’t need to have everything figured out.

Brian：对。更重要的信息是，不用事先把所有事情都想明白。

Ava: That sounds almost too good to be true for an open source call.

Ava：对开源项目征集来说，这听着好得有点不真实。

What does “not everything figured out” mean here?

这里的“不用把所有事情都想明白”具体是什么意思？

Brian: Academic research software and sustainable community projects are often built under different conditions.

Brian：学术研究软件和可持续的社区项目，往往是在不同条件下开发的。

Academic teams optimize for experimentation, publication, reproducibility, and research velocity.

学术团队更注重实验、发表论文、可复现性和研究进度。

A sustainable project also needs documentation, releases, governance, security, contributor onboarding, testing, licensing, maintainer succession, and community operations.

可持续的项目还需要文档、版本发布、治理、安全、帮助新贡献者上手、测试、许可证、维护者接班安排和社区运营。

Ava: Maintainer succession is a big one.

Ava：维护者接班是个大问题。

A student may create the project, graduate, and then nobody knows how to keep it moving.

学生创建项目后毕业了，接着没人知道该怎么把项目继续做下去。

Brian: Exactly. The article lists that situation directly.

Brian：没错。文章明确列举了这种情况。

Other examples include documentation that needs work, a repository that new contributors can’t navigate, an informal governance process, weak CI or packaging, or no CONTRIBUTING dot md, SECURITY dot md, or maintainer policy.

其他例子包括文档有待完善、新贡献者难以摸清代码仓库、治理流程不成文、持续集成或打包薄弱，或者缺少 CONTRIBUTING.md、SECURITY.md 或维护者政策。

Ava: So a project can apply even if those pieces are missing?

Ava：所以即使缺少这些，项目也可以申请？

Brian: Yes. Those gaps are part of the reason the workshop exists.

Brian：可以。这些短板正是举办工作坊的原因之一。

Teams may also be unsure how to build a contributor community, how to turn users into contributors, or what would eventually be needed to become a PyTorch Ecosystem project.

团队也可能不清楚如何建立贡献者社区、如何让用户成为贡献者，或将来成为 PyTorch Ecosystem 项目需要满足什么条件。

Ava: Let’s unpack the workshop itself. What happens during the project showcases?

Ava：我们具体聊聊工作坊。项目展示环节会做什么？

Brian: Selected teams explain the problem they’re solving, how they use or extend PyTorch Foundation hosted projects, and the academic context behind the work.

Brian：入选团队会说明自己要解决的问题、如何使用或扩展 PyTorch 基金会托管的项目，以及相关的学术背景。

They also talk about who uses the project today, why it could matter to the broader community, and what support or changes could help it reach the next stage.

他们还会介绍目前谁在使用项目、项目为何可能对更广泛的社区有价值，以及哪些支持或改进能帮助项目迈向下一阶段。

Ava: Then the office hours get more practical.

Ava：接下来，答疑时间会更注重实践。

Brian: Much more practical.

Brian：会实用得多。

Mentors may ask whether someone can understand what the project does, install it, run it, test it, and learn how to contribute.

导师可能会看，别人能否理解项目的用途，安装、运行和测试它，并学会如何参与贡献。

That’s what the article calls open source readiness.

文章把这称为“开源就绪度”。

Ava: I like that sequence. First, can a new user get started? Then, can a contributor join?

Ava：我喜欢这个顺序。先看新用户能否上手，再看贡献者能否参与。

Brian: Yes. Governance is another topic. Who makes decisions? Who can merge changes?

Brian：对。治理也是一个话题：谁做决定？谁有权合并修改？

What happens when the original academic maintainers move on?

最初的学术项目维护者离开后怎么办？

Ava: And reproducibility is broader than publishing a paper.

Ava：可复现性也不只是发表论文。

Brian: Right. Another researcher should be able to reproduce the results.

Brian：没错。其他研究者应该能够复现结果。

That means datasets, benchmarks, evaluation methodology, dependencies, and artifacts need to be clearly documented.

这意味着数据集、基准测试、评估方法、依赖项和成果文件都需要清楚记录。

Ava: What about the operational side?

Ava：运营方面呢？

Brian: They’ll discuss security and maintenance too: dependencies, vulnerabilities, releases, and supported PyTorch versions.

Brian：他们也会讨论安全和维护，包括依赖项、漏洞、版本发布，以及支持哪些 PyTorch 版本。

They may also look at ecosystem positioning.

他们可能还会考察项目在生态中的定位。

What’s the project’s relationship with PyTorch? What unique problem does it solve?

项目与 PyTorch 有什么关系？它解决了什么独特的问题？

What would need to change before a future PyTorch Ecosystem application made sense?

要做出哪些改变，今后申请加入 PyTorch Ecosystem 才合适？

Ava: That sounds like a roadmap conversation, not a pass-or-fail inspection.

Ava：听起来这是在讨论发展路线，而不是进行合格与否的检查。

Brian: That’s how the article presents it.

Brian：文章就是这么介绍的。

The ambition is to identify gaps and turn them into actionable next steps for making a project easier to use, contribute to, maintain, and grow.

目标是找出不足，制定可执行的后续步骤，让项目更易使用、贡献和维护，也更有发展空间。

Ava: Who can submit? Only professors or official university labs?

Ava：谁能提交申请？只有教授或大学的正式实验室吗？

Brian: No.

Brian：不是。

The call includes university students and graduate researchers, professors and academic educators, research labs and institutes, research software engineers, Academic OSPOs, maintainers of research-originated open source projects, and academic communities building tools around the hosted projects.

征集对象包括大学生和研究生、教授和高校教师、研究实验室和机构、科研软件工程师、学术机构开源项目办公室（Academic OSPO）、源于科研的开源项目维护者，以及围绕托管项目开发工具的学术社区。

Ava: And projects can come from anywhere in the world.

Ava：世界各地的项目都可以申请。

Brian: Yes.

Brian：对。

They don’t need to represent a large institution, have thousands of users, or already have a mature open source organization.

项目不必来自大型机构，不必有数千名用户，也不必已经建立成熟的开源组织。

The organizers care about the idea, its usefulness, its relationship with PyTorch, the work already happening around it, and its potential to grow beyond the original academic environment.

组织者看重项目的构想、实用性、与 PyTorch 的关系、现有工作，以及走出最初学术环境的发展潜力。

Ava: How will they evaluate submissions?

Ava：他们会如何评估申请？

Brian: There are six criteria.

Brian：有六项标准。

Academic relevance: the project came from, substantially grew out of, or supports academic research.

学术相关性：项目源于学术研究、在学术研究中得到显著发展，或为学术研究提供支持。

Ecosystem relevance: it uses, extends, or integrates with PyTorch or another hosted project.

生态相关性：项目使用、扩展或集成了 PyTorch 或其他托管项目。

Open source readiness: there’s a public repository, an open source license, and enough documentation for mentors to review it.

开源就绪度：项目有公开代码仓库、开源许可证，以及足以供导师评审的文档。

Ava: What are the remaining three?

Ava：剩下三项呢？

Brian: Potential impact, meaning it addresses a meaningful problem and could help users beyond the original research team.

Brian：潜在影响力，即项目解决了重要问题，也可能帮助原研究团队之外的用户。

Team commitment, meaning at least one maintainer can attend and continue the agreed next steps afterward.

团队投入度，即至少有一名维护者能参加活动，并在之后继续落实商定的后续步骤。

And cohort diversity, so the final group represents different institutions, research areas, project stages, and geographic regions.

还有入选项目的多样性，确保最终入选项目来自不同机构、研究领域、发展阶段和地区。

Ava: What should an applicant put in the submission?

Ava：申请材料里该写什么？

Brian: Start with the repository and a short description.

Brian：先提供代码仓库链接和简短介绍。

Explain where the project came from: a university, lab, research group, course, or academic collaboration.

说明项目的来源，比如大学、实验室、研究小组、课程或学术合作。

Then describe how it uses or extends PyTorch or its ecosystem, and who it helps, such as researchers, students, machine learning engineers, or a particular scientific community.

然后说明它如何使用或扩展 PyTorch 及其生态，以及它能帮助谁，比如研究者、学生、机器学习工程师或某个科学研究领域的群体。

Ava: They also want evidence of what exists today.

Ava：他们也想看到项目目前已有成果的证据。

Brian: Yes.

Brian：对。

Users, contributors, releases, papers, deployments, courses, benchmarks, or other signs of adoption are useful context.

用户、贡献者、发布版本、论文、实际部署、课程、基准测试或其他采用情况，都能提供有用的背景。

Then be honest about where you’re stuck: documentation, governance, packaging, testing, contributors, maintainers, visibility, reproducibility, or ecosystem readiness.

然后坦诚说明遇到的困难：文档、治理、打包、测试、贡献者、维护者、知名度、可复现性，或融入生态的准备情况。

Ava: And the final question is what the workshop should help achieve.

Ava：最后一个问题是，希望工作坊帮助实现什么。

Brian: Exactly. They’re not looking for perfectly polished applications.

Brian：没错。他们并不要求申请材料尽善尽美。

They’re looking for projects worth helping.

他们想找的是值得扶持的项目。

Ava: Let’s make the deadline clear. When does this call close?

Ava：把截止时间说清楚吧。这次征集何时结束？

Brian: September thirtieth, twenty twenty-six, at midnight Pacific Time.

Brian：太平洋时间 2026 年 9 月 30 日午夜。

Registration for the event is separate from the PyTorch Conference and will open later that week.

活动报名与 PyTorch 大会报名分开，将在当周晚些时候开放。

Ava: So if you have a project that deserves a life beyond a paper, thesis, course, grant, or research group, this is a direct invitation.

Ava：所以，如果你的项目值得在论文、学位论文、课程、资助项目或研究小组结束后继续发展，这就是一份直接的邀请。

Brian: Yes.

Brian：是的。

The project should already be open source and connected to PyTorch or one of the other hosted projects, but it doesn’t need to be polished.

项目应已开源，并与 PyTorch 或其他托管项目之一有关联，但不必打磨得很完善。

The workshop is designed for the transition itself.

这个工作坊就是为帮助项目完成这一转型而设的。

Ava: Let’s close with a three-point recap.

Ava：最后用三点总结一下。

First, this workshop helps academic PyTorch projects move toward sustainable community adoption.

第一，这个工作坊帮助学术界的 PyTorch 项目走向可持续的社区应用。

Brian: Second, selected teams get five-minute lightning talks, hands-on work with mentors, and office hours covering readiness, governance, reproducibility, community, security, maintenance, and ecosystem positioning.

Brian：第二，入选团队将进行五分钟闪电演讲，与导师一起动手实践，并参加答疑，讨论准备情况、治理、可复现性、社区、安全、维护和生态定位。

Ava: Third, the organizers value potential and commitment over perfect maturity.

Ava：第三，主办方更看重潜力和投入，而非项目是否已经尽善尽美。

Submit the project, explain what exists, say where you’re stuck, and describe the next step you want help with.

提交项目，说明已有的成果、遇到的难题，以及希望在哪个下一步获得帮助。

Brian: And remember the deadline: September thirtieth, twenty twenty-six, midnight Pacific Time.

Brian：别忘了截止时间：太平洋时间 2026 年 9 月 30 日午夜。

Ava: That’s it for today.

Ava：今天就到这里。

If your academic PyTorch project deserves a future beyond the original lab, bring it to the workshop.

如果你的学术 PyTorch 项目值得走出最初的实验室、继续发展，就带它来参加工作坊吧。

Brian: Thanks for listening. We’ll see you in the next episode.

Brian：感谢收听，我们下期见。
